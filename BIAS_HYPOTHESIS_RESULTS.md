# BIAS Hypothesis Results: Negative BIAS = Defensive Success

## Executive Summary

**Hypothesis:** Negative BIAS (defender closing the gap) is associated with defensive success (incomplete passes, interceptions).

**Result:** ✅ **HYPOTHESIS STRONGLY SUPPORTED**

---

## Key Findings

### Overall Results

- **Defensive Success Mean BIAS:** -0.74 yards (defenders close the gap)
- **Offensive Success Mean BIAS:** -0.18 yards (less gap closing)
- **Difference:** -0.56 yards (statistically significant, p < 0.001)

### Negative BIAS Frequency

- **Defensive Success:** 65.1% of plays have negative BIAS
- **Offensive Success:** 55.5% of plays have negative BIAS
- **Difference:** 9.6 percentage points (statistically significant, p < 0.001)

**Interpretation:** Defenders close the gap more frequently and by a larger amount on incomplete/intercepted passes compared to completed passes.

## Statistical Significance

### T-Test (Mean BIAS Difference):
- **t-statistic:** -5.07
- **p-value:** < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Conclusion:** Defensive success has significantly more negative BIAS

### Chi-Square Test (Negative BIAS Frequency):
- **Chi-square statistic:** 18.15
- **p-value:** < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Conclusion:** Negative BIAS is significantly more common on defensive success

## Hypothesis Conclusion

### ✅ HYPOTHESIS STRONGLY SUPPORTED

**Evidence:**
1. **Mean BIAS is more negative on defensive success** (-0.74 vs -0.18 yards)
2. **Higher percentage of negative BIAS on defensive success** (65.1% vs 55.5%)
3. **Statistically significant differences** (p < 0.001 for both tests)
4. **Consistent pattern across coverage types and throw depths**

**Interpretation:**
- When defenders successfully defend a pass (incomplete/intercepted), they consistently close the gap more than on completed passes
- This suggests that **defender reaction and gap-closing ability is a key factor in defensive success**
- The pattern holds across different coverage schemes and throw depths

**Practical Applications:**
- Defensive coordinators can use BIAS to evaluate coverage effectiveness
- Negative BIAS on defensive success indicates good defender reaction and closing speed
- Coverage types that show larger negative BIAS differences (Cover 3, Cover 2) may be more effective at closing gaps

## Key Takeaways

1. **Defenders close the gap more on defensive successes**
   - Average of 0.56 yards more gap closing on incomplete/intercepted passes

2. **Cover 3 Zone is most effective at closing gaps**
   - Shows -1.13 yards mean BIAS on defensive success
   - 70.9% of defensive successes have negative BIAS

3. **Deep passes show largest difference**
   - Defenders close 0.87 yards more on defensive success vs offensive success
   - Suggests deep coverage defenders react well to incomplete deep passes

4. **The pattern is consistent**
   - Holds across all major coverage types
   - Holds across all throw depths
   - Statistically significant and robust
